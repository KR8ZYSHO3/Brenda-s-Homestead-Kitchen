    Pricing basics

    Understand your food costs

    Know what a batch costs you before you pick a selling price. Read the example, do the practice exercise, then use the calculator for each real item you sell.

    Why this matters

    • If you only guess at prices, you can sell a lot and still lose money.
    • Food cost is what the ingredients (and packaging for that batch) cost you.
    • Your selling price should cover food cost, a little for your time, and a bit of profit.
    • Soap works the same way — oils, lye, fragrance, and the wrapper all count.

    Simple 3-step method

    1. Batch cost — add up what you spent for this bake (or soap batch).
    2. Per-unit cost — divide batch cost by how many cookies, jars, loaves, or bars you got.
    3. Selling price — start around 2–3× the per-unit cost (or more if your time is high), then adjust for what neighbors will pay.

    Rule of thumb only — McArthur market prices and your time matter too.

    Ohio guide

    Foods & soap rules basics

    Plain-English overview for Brenda. This is educational, not legal advice — check Ohio Department of Agriculture / FDA guidance if unsure.

    Two product types on this site

    • Baked goods & foods — follow Ohio cottage food rules (labels, shelf-stable list, Ohio sales).
    • Soap — not covered by cottage food law. True handmade soap (oils + lye, sold only as soap) is usually treated differently from cosmetics.
    • In Admin → Products, pick a category for each item so shoppers can filter Foods vs Soap.
    • Both types are fully editable: name, price, quantity, photo, ingredients list, and availability.

    Soap notes (important)

    • Soap is not an Ohio cottage food product.
    • If a bar is mainly oils + lye (alkali salts of fatty acids) and you market it only as soap for washing, it often falls outside FDA cosmetic rules (CPSC territory for true soap).
    • If you claim moisturizing, anti-aging, acne treatment, germ-killing, etc., or use synthetic detergent bases, it may be regulated as a cosmetic (or even a drug).
    • Ohio notes that cosmetics made at home are generally exempt from ODA inspection, but must be properly labeled and not adulterated. Commercial cosmetic sites may need ODA inspection.
    • Keep ingredient lists for every soap. Avoid disease/treatment claims on labels or the website.

    The good news (cottage foods)

    • Ohio lets you sell many shelf-stable homemade foods from your home kitchen.
    • For standard cottage foods: no state license, no inspection, and no sales dollar cap.
    • You can sell direct to customers in Ohio (markets, pickup, local events, and often retail within Ohio when properly labeled).
    • Your town is McArthur, Ohio — keep sales inside Ohio.

    What you can sell (examples)

    • Baked goods that do not need refrigeration (cookies, breads, many cakes/pies that are shelf-stable)
    • Jams, jellies, fruit butters
    • Candy, granola, popcorn, dry mixes
    • Only non-potentially-hazardous (shelf-stable) foods on Ohio’s approved list

    Not allowed under cottage food: foods that need fridge/freezer to stay safe, acidified/low-acid canned foods, and other potentially hazardous foods.

    Every label must include

    • Business / producer name and physical address
    • Product name
    • Ingredients in order by weight
    • Net weight (U.S. and metric is safest)
    • Major allergens (milk, eggs, wheat, peanuts, tree nuts, soy, fish, shellfish, sesame, etc.)
    • Exact statement in at least 10-point type: This product is home produced.

    Do you pay taxes? Yes.

    • Your cottage food income is taxable. Cottage law removes food-license hurdles — it does not remove income tax.
    • Report profit on federal Schedule C (sole proprietor).
    • If net self-employment income is over about $400, self-employment tax usually applies.
    • Ohio income tax can apply; most take-home cottage foods are not charged Ohio sales tax when sold for off-premises eating.
    • Use the Taxes & expenses tab to track income and costs for your accountant or tax software.

    Practical checklist before selling

    • Confirm each recipe is an approved shelf-stable cottage food
    • Print compliant labels for every package
    • Sell only within Ohio
    • Set Venmo / Cash App / PayPal / Zelle handles in Settings
    • Keep order and expense records for tax time
    • Ask Vinton County zoning/local rules if selling from home raises questions

    Helpful official places

    • Ohio Revised Code cottage food sections (around ORC 3715.01 / 3715.023 / 3715.025)
    • Ohio Administrative Code 901:3-20 (cottage food rules / allowed foods)
    • Ohio Department of Agriculture — Food Safety

    When in doubt, call ODA food safety or a local advisor before selling a new product type.

      Simple instructions

      How to use this website

      Products

      Go to Products & photos. Click Add product. Choose category Baked goods & foods or Soap. Fill in name, price, quantity on hand, ingredients, allergens/sensitivities, and upload a picture. Turn Available on so shoppers can order it. Edit anytime — soaps work the same way as baked goods.

      Google Order Form

      Go to Google Order Form. Create the form in Google with the starter questions, paste the share link, and Save. Customers then order on the website through that Google Form. Edit questions anytime in Google — use the Edit link saved here.

      Orders (manual / market)

      For phone, text, or market sales, use Orders & quantities → Manual order so stock and tax totals stay accurate. Mark “Paid” when Venmo/Cash App money arrives. Google Form replies also arrive in your Gmail / Sheets.

      Payments

      In Settings, enter your Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, and Zelle handles. They appear on the order page so customers know where to send money.

      Taxes

      Log expenses when you buy supplies. At tax time, pick the year and export CSV. Your sales totals and expenses are ready to hand to whoever does your taxes.

      Backup

      In Settings, click Download backup every week or after a busy market day. This saves your products, orders, and expenses as a file on your computer.

      Data is stored in this browser on this computer/phone. Use the same browser for admin work, and download backups. If you later want phone + laptop sharing in the cloud, we can upgrade hosting.
